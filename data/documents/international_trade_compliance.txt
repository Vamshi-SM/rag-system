BACKGROUND
Falcon Aerospace collaborates with NovaTech Systems on Project
Falconstrike, an autonomous navigation system with potential dual-use
applications in both commercial drone platforms and defense-adjacent
unmanned aerial systems. This review assesses export control
classification and compliance obligations arising from Falcon Aerospace's
international sales and technology-sharing activities related to the
project, and its intersection with the cybersecurity incident described
in cybersecurity_incident_report.txt.

SCOPE OF REVIEW
1. Export Control Classification Number (ECCN) determination for
   Falconstrike navigation modules.
2. International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) applicability
   assessment.
3. Review of technology transfer agreements with foreign entities,
   including a pending licensing discussion with a European drone
   manufacturer.
4. Denied Parties List (DPL) and Entity List screening procedures.

FINDINGS
1. ECCN Classification: The Falconstrike navigation module was classified
   under ECCN 7A611 (military electronics) in a 2024 classification
   request, but recent design modifications developed jointly with
   NovaTech Systems may warrant reclassification review, potentially to
   ECCN 7A994 (less restrictive, dual-use category).
2. ITAR Applicability: Falcon Aerospace's current licenses under the
   International Traffic in Arms Regulations (22 C.F.R. Parts 120-130)
   remain valid through December 2027 (License No. DDTC-2024-118820);
   however, the scope of the Falconstrike collaboration was not
   explicitly addressed in the original license application.
3. Technology Transfer Agreements: A pending technology licensing
   discussion with European drone manufacturer AeroVantage GmbH requires
   further export control review before any technical data disclosure
   occurs, given the potential ECCN 7A611 classification.
4. Screening Procedures: DPL and Entity List screening protocols were
   found to be functioning appropriately; no matches identified in
   sampled transactions for the review period.
5. Following the March 18, 2026 cybersecurity incident affecting Project
   Falconstrike source code (see cybersecurity_incident_report.txt),
   Nadia Petrov flagged a potential deemed export and unauthorized
   disclosure concern requiring further investigation.
