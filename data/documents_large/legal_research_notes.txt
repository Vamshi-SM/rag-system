MEMO 1: GDPR AND HIPAA INTERPLAY FOR TELEHEALTH SERVICES
===============================================================================
Matter Number: APX-2024-0188
Client: Redwood Healthcare
Date: 2024-04-22
Related Document: Client Consultation Notes, Entry 3 (Redwood Healthcare -
Data Privacy Consultation, 2024-04-11)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. QUESTION PRESENTED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Whether Redwood Healthcare's planned telehealth platform expansion
("Project Meridian"), which will process personal data of patients
located in the European Union, triggers obligations under both the
General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Health Insurance
Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), and what cross-border
transfer mechanisms are required.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. SHORT ANSWER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes. Because Redwood Healthcare will offer telehealth services to
individuals located in the European Union, GDPR Article 3(2) extends
GDPR's territorial scope to this processing regardless of Redwood
Healthcare's lack of an EU establishment. Simultaneously, because Redwood
Healthcare is a HIPAA-covered entity, all protected health information
(PHI) collected through the platform remains subject to HIPAA's Privacy
and Security Rules. Cross-border transfers of EU patient data to
Redwood Healthcare's U.S.-based servers require a valid transfer
mechanism under GDPR Chapter V, most practically Standard Contractual
Clauses (SCCs).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. ANALYSIS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. GDPR Territorial Scope (Article 3(2))
GDPR Article 3(2) applies to the processing of personal data of data
subjects who are in the European Union by a controller or processor not
established in the EU, where the processing activities relate to the
offering of goods or services to such data subjects. Redwood Healthcare's
telehealth platform, by accepting EU-based patients, falls squarely
within this provision.

B. Cross-Border Transfer Mechanisms (GDPR Chapter V, Articles 44-49)
Article 44 prohibits transfers of personal data to third countries
(including the United States) unless an adequate transfer mechanism is
in place. Following the Court of Justice of the European Union's Schrems
II decision, reliance on adequacy decisions for U.S. transfers is
limited; Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), updated per the European
Commission's 2021 SCC modules, remain the most practical mechanism for
Redwood Healthcare's use case. Recommend Module Two (Controller to
Processor) clauses for the relationship with cloud hosting vendor
BluePeak Software.

Note: Due diligence performed in connection with NovaTech Systems'
unrelated acquisition of BluePeak Software (Matter Number APX-2024-0201,
Finding DD-010) identified that BluePeak Software's existing data
processing addenda lack SCCs for transfers to its Canadian subsidiary.
This is a separate but related gap that should be resolved in
coordination between the Redwood Healthcare and NovaTech Systems
engagement teams, given the shared vendor.

C. HIPAA Business Associate Agreement Requirements
Under 45 CFR 164.502(e) and 164.504(e), Redwood Healthcare must maintain
a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with any vendor that creates,
receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on its behalf. As identified in
the April 11, 2024 client consultation, gaps exist with three
subcontractors: BluePeak Software, MedConnect Data Systems, and CarePath
Analytics. All three BAAs must be executed or updated prior to the
Project Meridian launch.

D. Interplay and Practical Recommendation
Where GDPR and HIPAA both apply, the more protective standard generally
should govern for overlapping data elements. Recommend Redwood Healthcare
adopt a unified data protection addendum with vendors that incorporates
both HIPAA BAA requirements and GDPR-compliant SCCs, to avoid maintaining
duplicative and potentially inconsistent contractual frameworks.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. CONCLUSION AND NEXT STEPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Execute updated BAAs with BluePeak Software, MedConnect Data Systems,
   and CarePath Analytics prior to July 1, 2024 launch date.
2. Implement SCC Module Two with BluePeak Software, addressing the
   Canadian subsidiary transfer gap.
3. Complete Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) by June 1, 2024
   per Client Consultation Notes Entry 3, Action Item 1.

MEMO 2: SECTION 14.2 LATE DELIVERY REMEDIES - INTERPRETIVE ANALYSIS
===============================================================================
Matter Number: APX-2024-0113
Client: NovaTech Systems, Inc.
Case ID: LC-2045
Date: 2024-02-15

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. QUESTION PRESENTED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Whether Section 14.2 of the Master Services Agreement between NovaTech
Systems and GreenStone Logistics (Contract No. MSA-NT-GS-2022-0087)
permits recovery of consequential damages, including lost sales and
customer chargebacks, or whether such damages are excluded by the
agreement's limitation of liability clause (Section 20.1).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. SHORT ANSWER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 14.2 provides a specific liquidated remedy structure for late
deliveries (a per-shipment fee schedule) that operates independently of
the general limitation of liability in Section 20.1. Because Section
20.1 contains a carve-out for damages arising from Section 14.2 late
delivery events ("notwithstanding anything to the contrary in Section
20.1, remedies under Section 14.2 shall not be subject to the exclusion
of consequential damages"), NovaTech Systems has a strong argument that
its consequential damages (lost sales, chargebacks) tied to specific late
delivery events remain recoverable, though the liquidated fee schedule
in 14.2(b) may function as an additional (not exclusive) remedy
depending on how the agreement's "sole remedy" language in 14.2(d) is
construed.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. ANALYSIS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The interpretive tension is between Section 14.2(d), which states the
liquidated fee schedule "shall be Client's sole and exclusive remedy for
late deliveries," and Section 20.1's carve-out language, which appears
to preserve broader damages specifically for Section 14.2 events. Under
general contract interpretation principles, specific provisions
(14.2(d)) generally govern over general carve-outs (20.1) when there is
a direct conflict; however, courts applying Illinois law (the governing
law clause, Section 24.1, designates Illinois) have held that an
express carve-out referencing a specific section by number, as here,
can be read as the parties' intent to exclude that section from an
otherwise-applicable "sole remedy" limitation elsewhere in the same
section, creating ambiguity that may need to be resolved via extrinsic
evidence of negotiation history.

Recommend obtaining the contract negotiation file (email correspondence
between the parties during the 2022 MSA negotiation) to determine
whether drafts show an intent to preserve consequential damages
alongside the liquidated fee schedule.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. CONCLUSION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NovaTech Systems has a colorable, though not airtight, argument for
recovering consequential damages under Section 14.2 in addition to any
liquidated fees. This analysis supports the litigation team's decision
to plead both a liquidated damages theory and a broader consequential
damages theory in the alternative. See Litigation Case Notes (Case ID
LC-2045) for how this analysis was incorporated into case strategy.

MEMO 3: ISO 27001 CERTIFICATION AND CONTRACTUAL REPRESENTATIONS
===============================================================================
Matter Number: APX-2024-0233
Client: Summit Energy Group
Date: 2024-08-05

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. QUESTION PRESENTED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What contractual representations, if any, should Summit Energy Group
make to counterparties regarding its ISO 27001 certification status
while the company is in the process of recertification (anticipated
completion Q4 2024)?

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. SHORT ANSWER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Summit Energy Group should avoid representing that it is "currently
ISO 27001 certified" in any contract executed before recertification is
complete, and should instead use language such as "maintains an
information security management system aligned with ISO 27001
standards and is undergoing recertification, expected to be completed
by [date]." This avoids a potential misrepresentation claim if
recertification is delayed or identifies findings requiring remediation.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. ANALYSIS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Contractual representations of current certification status create risk
if the certifying body (in this case, a third-party ISO certification
auditor engaged through Sterling Consulting) identifies nonconformities
during the recertification audit that delay issuance of the renewed
certificate. See Compliance Audit Report (Matter Number APX-2024-0233)
for Sterling Consulting's preliminary audit findings, which identified
one High Risk finding related to access control logging that must be
remediated before recertification can be completed.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. CONCLUSION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recommend Summit Energy Group's Legal & Compliance department (Kevin
Walsh, VP) circulate updated representation language to all business
development personnel prior to any contract renewals during Q3-Q4 2024.
